CHUA, a synthesizer made of one chaotic circuit

Two capacitors, an inductor, a resistor and one nonlinear part. That is enough for the state to spiral around one equilibrium, get thrown to the other, and never repeat. Each voice of the FM-1 firmware runs its own copy of this circuit; the knobs below are the same knobs, mapped through the same tables.

The equations

Three state variables, one bent line

Made dimensionless, the circuit is three coupled equations. x is the voltage over C1, y the voltage over C2, z the current through the inductor. All the chaos comes from g(x), the Chua diode's current: a negative resistance that pumps energy in near zero.

dx/dt = α (y − x − g(x))
dy/dt = x − y + z
dz/dt = −β y
g(x) slope −8/7 for |x| < 1, −5/7 to |x| = 3, +2 beyond (the op-amps saturate)
pitch: the solver advances h = fnote · Trotation / 44 100 per sample
On the FM-1

The same circuit, eight times, in fixed point

Each voice integrates its own circuit with the midpoint method in Q20 fixed point, at most four substeps per sample. The regime landmarks, rotation times and loudness per setting were measured offline; this page uses the same numbers. The screen draws the newest voice's trajectory, like the scope above.
